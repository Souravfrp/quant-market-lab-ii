# Where the forecast experiment stands on 7 October

Status recorded on 7 October 2026, before that day's US regular close. My observation cutoff for this note is the 6 October close. This dated note remains a record of what was available then; a later evaluation belongs in a separate dated file.

## My three different dates

I distinguish the information cutoff, forecast creation/publication, and the dates used to measure the outcome. A model can use August data and still be created after a September outcome. The earlier input cutoff does not turn that new calculation into a prospective September forecast.

| Record | Inputs / origin | Outcome dates | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical May–August checks | 30 April | Four saved delayed-gap windows ending by 7 August | Historical diagnostics; not a daily or whole-month risk series |
| September record | 31 August | 1, 2, 3, 4, 8 September | Retrospective; predictions were created 30 September |
| Original October record | 31 August | 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 October; anchored at 30 September | Frozen baseline forecast; final outcome pending in this note |
| October partial observation | Through 6 October | 1, 2, 5, 6 October | Four completed returns only; not the final five-return target |
| New signal-processing plan | Recorded 7 October | Historical comparison first, later forecast separately | Plan only; no new forecast or fitted signal model |

The November and December baseline records also remain in the frozen archive. I do not overwrite any of these predictions when I improve the method.

## What can be measured through 6 October

I keep five observed prices in a [separate evaluation CSV](../results/evaluation_inputs/spy_october_partial_2026-10-06.csv): 30 September plus 1, 2, 5 and 6 October. I transcribed the displayed adjusted closes from Stock Analysis, and checked the historical close column on ChartExchange. Their prices agree on these dates. The [provenance record](../results/evaluation_inputs/spy_october_partial_2026-10-06_provenance.json) identifies the sources, date, rounding and limitations.

This public snapshot is a different source/vintage from my original frozen training file. It has two-decimal price precision. It is a partial observation, not a replacement for the training file or a final audited vendor dataset. I need one consistent adjusted-close snapshot covering all six required prices for the final October evaluation.

Define $S_4=\sum_{d\in\{1,2,5,6\}}r_{Oct\,d}^2$. The current descriptive RMS is $\sqrt{S_4/4}$. The original target is

$$Y_{Oct}=\sqrt{\frac{S_4+r_{Oct\,7}^2}{5}}.$$

Four-return RMS and five-return RMS have different denominators and represent different windows. I do not calculate final forecast errors from the partial RMS. I can state the mathematical lower bound $Y_{Oct}\ge\sqrt{S_4/5}$, subject to this snapshot's precision, but it is not a completed actual and it is not a confidence interval. The pending return is not filled with zero.

## When the final October actual becomes available

I need the adjusted close dated 7 October, after the regular session. The regular close is 16:00 New York time, which on this date corresponds to 01:30 IST on 8 October. I also wait for the provider to publish the completed adjusted-close record. I use the closes for 30 September, 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 October to calculate the five daily returns. Waiting four additional days is not required by this target.

I then save the six-price input, provenance, RMS, and errors against the three original forecasts in a new evaluation record. I leave the frozen forecast files and August training snapshot alone. One October window gives three forecast errors, not evidence of general model superiority.

## What a new model created today can claim

A signal model created now for October 1–7 is a partially observed-window experiment: four of its five returns are already available. It is not a fully prospective five-return forecast, even if I restrict its inputs to August. I can evaluate it historically with its actual creation date recorded. For a prospective signal experiment I need a later window and a separately published forecast before that window's anchoring close, consistent with my protocol.

A forecast of only the next daily return would be a different target and needs a separate specification; it does not replace my five-return RMS experiment.

## Reproduce this figure

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements-figures.txt
python -m src.plot_evaluation_status
```

The script reads saved CSVs only. It makes no network requests, does not fit models, and writes this dated PNG and derived partial-October tables. To keep the dates auditable, it requires exactly the expected five-price partial snapshot and the original frozen forecast windows. The code sorts the $M$ saved comparison rows in $O(M\log M)$ time and uses $O(M)$ storage; the four-return calculation itself is constant-size. Sources are in [references](references.md); the new signal equations, choices and computational costs are in [the plan](signal_processing_plan.md).
